## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. Standardising DeFi protocol UX into a reusable library (Design systems at scale)
**S:** Rome Blockchain Labs was launching new client protocols one after another (Tashi Finance, Hover Market, kinetic.market, Bits.Financial, Sceptre.fi, Trad.fi), and each new protocol risked reinventing lending, borrowing, staking and yield-farming interaction patterns from scratch.
**T:** As design lead for the firm's entire DeFi portfolio, standardise these interaction patterns so new protocols could launch on proven flows instead of bespoke design work.
**A:** Architected end-to-end design systems across 7 live DeFi & RWA protocols, extracting the recurring lending/borrowing/staking/yield-farming patterns into a reusable protocol UX library, and worked directly with engineering and founders to validate technical feasibility of each pattern.
**R:** Each new client protocol shipped on proven, tested flows rather than bespoke design, cutting design and handoff time on subsequent launches.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you built something for scale", "How do you approach design systems?", "Describe a time you reduced duplicated effort across a team"

### 2. Integrating AI-assisted prototyping into the product workflow (Process innovation / AI-native building)
**S:** Design-to-engineering handoff on complex DeFi products created ambiguity, and moving from concept to a testable prototype traditionally took weeks.
**T:** Find a way to compress that cycle and reduce handoff ambiguity with engineering.
**A:** Integrated Claude Code, Claude Design, Figma Make and Lovable into the product workflow, taking concepts from wireframe to functional React/Tailwind prototypes directly, rather than static mockups alone.
**R:** Cut prototyping time from weeks to days and reduced handoff ambiguity with engineering, since engineers could inspect a working prototype instead of interpreting static designs.
**Use for:** "How do you use AI tools in your work?", "Tell me about a process you improved", "Describe a time you introduced a new tool or way of working"

### 3. Owning a product from zero at CITIZEN (Ambiguity / ownership)
**S:** CITIZEN was a new venture with no existing brand, design system, or product roadmap.
**T:** As UX/UI Designer & Product Owner, define the brand identity, design system and roadmap that would give the venture a distinct market identity, while aligning with commercial goals.
**A:** Led market research with the marketing team to size target audiences, then designed the customer-facing interfaces and contributed to the business plan directly, feeding research findings back into product decisions.
**R:** Delivered a distinct brand and product identity and a validated roadmap grounded in real market research, from a standing start.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you had to build something with no existing structure", "Describe your experience owning a product end to end", "How do you handle ambiguity?"

### 4. Digital transformation for Marinha Grande City Hall (Stakeholder management / public-sector delivery)
**S:** As senior advisor to Marinha Grande's Mayor, Raul led the advisory team responsible for modernising the city's brand and digital public services, including for the Portuguese National Glass Museum.
**T:** Design and implement a new city brand, design system, and public digital services, including the city's main app and an augmented reality app for the museum, through a market-research-driven process.
**A:** Idealized, designed and implemented the new brand and design system, led the advisory team, and drove a market-research-oriented process to shape the city's main app and the museum's AR app.
**R:** Delivered a new city brand, design system, and two shipped public-facing digital products (city app and AR museum app), used by residents and museum visitors.
**Use for:** "Tell me about working with non-technical or public-sector stakeholders", "Describe a project with a long delivery cycle", "How do you translate research into a shipped product?"

### 5. Building the Lobos Sports Management Platform solo with AI (Technical initiative / full-stack AI building)
**S:** Leiria's Wolves Club, a rugby and martial arts school Raul founded, needed athlete registration, class scheduling and payments handled digitally, with no engineering budget or team.
**T:** Design and build a working, full-stack club management app without a traditional engineering team.
**A:** Designed and deployed the app end-to-end using Lovable AI, building athlete registration, class scheduling and payments as in-app flows, bridging real community operations with rapid software prototyping.
**R:** The platform is now in daily use at Leiria's Wolves Club, serving 250 users.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you built something outside your usual scope", "How far can you take a product with AI tools alone?", "Describe a personal project you're proud of"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why are you looking to leave Rome Blockchain Labs?"
> After leading design across 7 live DeFi & RWA protocols and standardising the firm's protocol UX patterns into a reusable library, I'm looking for a role where I can apply that same AI-native, systems-first approach at a broader scale, either deeper into design leadership or across a wider product surface than a single firm's client portfolio.

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience, e.g. production engineering]."
> My AI-assisted workflow with Claude Code and Lovable lets me take a design all the way to a functional prototype, which is closer to engineering than traditional design handoff, but I'm not claiming to be a production engineer. I partner closely with engineering to validate feasibility and ship, and I'm upfront about where that partnership needs to continue.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Growing into a design leadership role, most likely Head of Design or Design Director, where I'm setting design process and culture for a team, not just shipping my own work. Building AI-native workflows into how that team operates would be part of that.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> I move fast on design decisions once I have a clear read on the problem, which is a strength when speed matters, but on ambiguous or high-stakes calls I've learned to slow down and validate with user research first rather than defaulting to intuition. That's part of why I built usability testing and research into the retention-redesign work at Rome Blockchain Labs rather than skipping straight to execution.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
